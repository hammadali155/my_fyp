Computational Architectures for Classical Arabic and Quranic Linguistics: A Comprehensive Reference and Literature ReviewThe digitization of Classical Arabic (CA) and Quranic studies represents one of the most complex frontiers in Natural Language Processing (NLP). Unlike Modern Standard Arabic (MSA), Classical Arabic demands a higher threshold of precision due to its liturgical significance, where a single diacritic error can fundamentally alter theological meaning. This report delineates the theoretical and practical landscape for an integrated learning platform, synthesizing established tools with emerging research in morphology, syntax, artificial intelligence, and cognitive science.The Morphological Landscape: Sarf and Lexical DisambiguationThe foundation of any Arabic linguistic platform rests on its ability to handle morphological richness. Arabic is a root-and-pattern language where words are formed by interspersing a semantic root (usually three consonants) with a morphosyntactic pattern. This structure results in a high degree of inflectional and derivational complexity.Evolution of Morphological AnalyzersThe integration of CAMeL Tools provides a robust baseline for Modern Standard Arabic, yet the nuances of Classical Arabic necessitate the inclusion of complementary systems like MADAMIRA and Farasa to ensure coverage of archaic forms and complex clitic attachments. MADAMIRA, developed through collaboration between Columbia and New York University Abu Dhabi, remains a vital reference point. It combines the strengths of MADA (Morphological Analysis and Disambiguation for Arabic) and AMIRA to provide diacritization, lemmatization, and POS tagging with high accuracy.MADAMIRA’s architecture utilizes Support Vector Machines (SVMs) and N-gram language models to resolve ambiguities in undiacritized text. In the context of the Holy Quran, where words often contain multiple attached particles (e.g., fasayakfīkahum), Farasa’s segmentation logic offers a complementary lens to CAMeL Tools, particularly in identifying the boundaries of clitics that are crucial for introductory learners.The Challenge of DiacritizationDiacritization (Harakaat) serves as the primary mechanism for resolving lexical and syntactic ambiguity in Arabic. While modern text is largely undiacritized, Classical Arabic pedagogy requires full vocalization to guide the learner's pronunciation and understanding of case endings (I’rab). Recent research suggests that the most effective way to improve automatic diacritization is through the integration of syntactic analysis.CAMeL lab researchers have pioneered techniques that combine handwritten rules for case assignment with machine learning models trained on the output of morphological taggers. This hybrid approach is essential for a learning platform because it ensures that the diacritics displayed to the student are not just statistically likely but grammatically correct according to the rules of Nahw. Furthermore, the use of diacritization-to-annotation techniques allows for the creation of high-quality training data at a lower cost, which can be leveraged to fine-tune AI models for the Quranic domain.Syntactic Frameworks and the Advancement of TreebankingIn the study of Nahw, the transition from identifying words to understanding sentence structure requires sophisticated syntactic parsing. The field has moved from simple rule-based top-down or bottom-up parsing toward deep dependency frameworks.From the Quranic Arabic Corpus to the Extended Quranic TreebankWhile the Quranic Arabic Corpus (QAC) has long been the standard for online Quranic linguistics, it suffers from significant limitations in the modern computational era. Specifically, the QAC provides coverage for only approximately 40% of the Quran and presents its syntactic data in a format that is not easily processable by modern NLP pipelines.The emergence of the Extended Quranic Treebank (EQTB) addresses these gaps. It provides a landmark contribution by offering the first complete, computationally accessible, and meticulously annotated treebank for the entire Quran. The inclusion of a "TAQDIR" module to handle elliptical constructions—where words are implied but not present—is particularly revolutionary for teaching advanced Quranic nuances.Dependency Parsing and Semantic RelatednessTo provide interactive feedback, the platform must employ real-time parsers. Beyond the integrated Farasa parser, CamelParser represents a state-of-the-art system for Arabic syntactic analysis. Research indicates that fine-tuning transformer models like AraBERTv2 is currently the most effective method for capturing syntactic dependencies in morphologically rich languages like Arabic.Furthermore, the integration of semantic similarity models is crucial for helping students understand thematic connections within the Quran. The QurSim dataset, when used to fine-tune AraBERT, allows for the classification of verse pairs based on semantic relatedness. In Classical Arabic, an incorrect vowel (Haraka) can change a word from the active to the passive voice, potentially altering a divine command.To address this, the IslamicEval 2025 shared task focused on identifying and verifying Quranic and Hadith quotes in LLM-generated text. The winning approach for such tasks often involves Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). By grounding the Gemini tutor in a verified knowledge base—such as the Tanzil Quranic dataset or the newly developed EQTB—the system can verify its output before presenting it to the user.A hybrid RAG system for this platform should combine:Sparse Retrieval (TF-IDF): Excellent for identifying exact word matches in Quranic verses.Dense Retrieval (Bi-Encoders): Better at capturing semantic intent in student queries.Cross-Encoder Reranking: To ensure the most contextually relevant verse is retrieved for the AI tutor to explain.Memory Optimization: The FSRS AlgorithmThe SRS component of the platform is vital for vocabulary and grammar retention. While SM-2 (the algorithm behind Anki) has been the industry standard for decades, the Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler (FSRS) represents a significant leap forward in memory modeling.Mathematical Foundations of FSRSFSRS is a modern algorithm optimized using machine learning to improve long-term retention and scheduling efficiency. Unlike SM-2, which uses fixed formulas to determine intervals, FSRS models three key memory factors: stability (S), retrieval strength (R), and the spacing effect.The retrieval strength is modeled by an exponential forgetting curve:$$R = e^{\ln(0.9) \cdot \frac{t}{S}}$$
where $t$ is the time since the last review and $S$ is the stability of the memory.
